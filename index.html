— Sections in development

Four tools, one job: get pilots to "I understand."

01 / Learn

Concepts mapped to the ACS

Every weather concept the ACS expects you to know — fronts, stability, icing, fog, thunderstorms, turbulence — explained the way a tutor would explain it. With visuals, and with what the examiner actually wants to hear.

In progress · Phase 2
02 / Decoder

METAR & TAF translator

Paste a raw METAR or TAF. Get a plain-English breakdown plus a visual: cloud layers at altitude, wind arrow over a runway, visibility ring. The way you wish you'd been taught it the first time.

Planned · Phase 3
03 / Nav Log

Cross-country, walked through

A guided nav log builder. Enter your route, pick an altitude, the tool pulls winds aloft and computes WCA, ground speed, ETE, and fuel burn per leg. For students drowning in their first XC plan.

Planned · Phase 4
04 / ACS

The standard, looked up fast

The weather-related ACS task elements for Private, Instrument, and Commercial. Search by topic, see the standard, jump straight to the relevant explainer. No more PDF-scrolling at 11 PM the night before.

Planned · Phase 5

— Why this exists

Most pilots don't fail weather questions because they're bad at weather.

They fail because the products were designed for a 1970s teletype and have never been re-translated for visual learners. As a meteorology tutor and student pilot working through the ratings, I see the same six concepts trip people up over and over. CrosswindWX is the site I wish I'd had when I started studying for my own check-ride.
